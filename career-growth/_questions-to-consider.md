# Career Growth — Questions to Consider

Use these questions when exploring the notes in this folder. They are not meant to be answered all at once — pick one or two that create friction.

## Honest self-assessment

- What is the gap between how I see my own performance and how others describe it?
- Which past successes am I still coasting on instead of building new ones?
- What uncomfortable feedback have I received more than once and not fully acted on?

## Growth blockers

- What am I avoiding that I know would accelerate my growth?
- What would I need to believe about myself to operate one level above where I am today?
- Where am I optimizing for comfort rather than compounding?

## Looking ahead

- Who is on a path I want to be on — and what specifically are they doing differently?
- What skill, if I invested in it now, would pay the most dividends in three years?
- When did I last put myself in a situation where failure was genuinely possible?

## Problem framing and self-honesty

- What problem are we actually trying to solve? (Not what solution do we want, but what's the underlying problem?)
- Who's the user here and what's painful for them? (Be specific — "users" isn't an answer.)
- What are we assuming that might be wrong? (Every plan has hidden assumptions.)
- What happens if we're wrong and ship this anyway? (How bad is the downside?)
- If I just kept showing up and doing the work, would it eventually get done? If yes, it's hard. If more work wouldn't make a dent, or the situation keeps shifting underneath you, it's probably complex.
- Examine your own incentive structures carefully. What alternative narratives for your behavior and beliefs could exist, especially if they contradict the heroic saga you're constructing for yourself? Carefully weighing "how might I be the villain in this story?" is an important and valuable skill.
