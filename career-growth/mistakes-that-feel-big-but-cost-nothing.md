# Mistakes That Feel Big But Cost Nothing

Here's a partial list of things people tend to avoid, at real cost to themselves, because they feel like large mistakes:

* Sending a rough draft too early. People will understand if it's incomplete; it's called a rough draft for a reason.  
* Asking a question that turns out to have an obvious answer. If you were wondering about it, so was someone else in the room, and now they're grateful you asked.  
* Guessing out loud in a meeting. If you label it a guess, that's what it is.  
* Proposing an idea that gets shot down in a brainstorm. That's the whole point of a brainstorm.  
* Shipping a prototype or first version with rough edges.  
* Admitting you don't understand something in a one-on-one. Telling them what you're stuck on or that you don't get things gives them an opening to help.  
* Applying for a job or a project you're not sure you're qualified for. If you don't get it, you're exactly where you were before.  
* Making content online. If nobody watches your videos or reads your articles, nobody watches your videos or reads your articles.  
* Following up with someone who hasn't replied. Most people are glad for a nudge.
