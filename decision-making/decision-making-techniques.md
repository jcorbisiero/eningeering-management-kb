### Decision Making Techniques

#### Decision Triangle

* Every decision has a context, constraints, scenarios, trigger, desired future, decision  
* Trigger: reason/why for making a decision  
* Desired future: the future the different stakeholders involved want  
* Context: include the internal (what you control) and external (what you can't control) context  
* Constraints: the circumstances in which we make our decision  
* Scenarios: document possible future scenarios  
* Analysis: research which scenario is most likely and why  
* Decision: the action you are taking

#### Pressing pause 

* General idea is to pause so that  
  * You can detach from emotions that blur your judgement at the moment  
  * You make sure that you can check for blind spots  
  * You get a new perspective  
  * You do not need to provide an immediate answer  
* Example: Dealing with triggering messages  
  * A message may come across as annoying or frustrating (triggering)  
  * Pausing will let you think process and think through the message  
  * Give enough time to wait to respond precisely and clearly  
* Example: Managing people who need answers  
  * If you don’t have an answer, pausing alone won’t help  
  * State clearly what you are in control of and what is outside of your control  
  * The important thing is to communicate all the actions you are taking, so they know you care about it

#### Gather, Decide, Execute

* Gather  
  * Gathering information is an active and ongoing process  
  * Information is raw data, while knowledge is information that has been processed and understood  
  * Build up a store of knowledge that you can use to make decisions, and this is done through active note-taking  
  * It's not about letting things come to you, actively be in the details and actively seeking out the information that you need to be effective  
  * Pull on threads and ask questions to learn more about the things that you need to know  
* Decide  
  * Proactively engage with the proposal and see it as an opportunity to further your understanding of the project, be more sure that we are making the right decisions, and therefore add value for yourself and the team  
  * Press on alternative design choices, ask for more detail on how this scales during peak traffic load  
  * Question anything that you consider to be an assumption  
* Execute  
  * Execute on the decisions and tasks that you've made for yourself  
  * Ensure that everything that you are accountable for via others is making progress

#### Making small bets

* Each sprint/iteration, an A/B test is a small bet \- in isolation they look small or not meaningful, but over time they compound  
* The best team isn't the one who wins big but the one who learns the fastest  
* If the odds are in your favor, bet big, if the odds are not bet small  
* If your culture is driven by evidence, instead you  
  * You test the riskiest assumptions first, before committing full resources  
  * You kill ideas fast if the data says it’s not working  
  * You double down only when there’s proof, not when there’s pressure  
* Wrong question \- “What’s the next big thing we’re going to ship?”  
* Better question \- “What’s the smallest possible bet we could make that teaches us something useful?”
