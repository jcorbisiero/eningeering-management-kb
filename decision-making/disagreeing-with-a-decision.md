# When you disagree with a decision

Consider:

> There were other options, and we discussed them. This is the direction we're taking, and here's how we'll make it successful.

vs a more empathetic:

> I understand this may not be your preferred path forward and you're not alone, but these concerns came up in the discussion and were given due consideration. This is where we landed, so let's give it our best.

## How it lands

When you hear: "This is what we're going to do" and that's the end of it, it sucks. It can feel like your objections are not being heard.

As a decision maker people will disagree, but offering what was considered, the trade-offs, and constraints helps explain how the decision was made. The person may still disagree which is ok, but they are more likely to commit to the work.

## Revisit the decision

- New information might change the trade-offs
- The plan may repeatedly run into friction once we try to implement it

Keep in mind, someone saying, "I still disagree" isn't sufficient reason to reopen a decision, but new information is. What specific problems are you seeing that change the calculus enough that we should revisit the conversation?

If you are the one doing the implementing, don't come back with "I told you so." Come back with "Here are three things we didn't anticipate, here's what they're costing us, and here's how they change the original trade-off."

## Alignment ≠ Agreement

Given all of the above, alignment doesn't mean absolute agreement.

- Do not leave your team in the dark in public
- Respect both the decision-making process and the people executing it
- Own the direction, do the work as if you were solely responsible for its success, and keep your eyes open for the genuine friction that proves it's time to revisit the decision
