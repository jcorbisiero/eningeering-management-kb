A bad decision leads to learning while no decision leads to nothing but frustration and meetings that drain valuable energy.


## Not every decision deserves the same energy

Map decisions on two axes — **reversibility** (hard → easy) and **impact** (low → high):

|                  | Hard to Reverse                                                                                      | Easy to Reverse                                                                              |
|------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **High Impact**  | **Evaluate carefully** — The only quadrant that earns deep analysis. Slow down, gather evidence, involve the right people. | **Decide and learn** — Make the call, ship it, watch what happens. The decision is the experiment. |
| **Low Impact**   | **Do you need that?** — Small today, permanent forever. These quietly pile into constraints. Skip most of them. | **Move on fast** — Deciding slowly costs more than deciding wrong. Pick one and keep moving. |

**Spend your energy where it can't be undone.**


## Four ways to make a decision

### Democratic
* The majority wins and the others accept
* Typically good for speed not quality
* Use democratic decisions for low-impact choices because speed wins

### Directive
* The boss decides and the others follow
* Works well when the person calling the shots knows the domain well and is an expert in the field
* Can limits teams’ creativity
* Use when you’re in a critical situation, where you have no time to discuss

### Consensus
* Everyone must agree before you move on
* Can be exhausting because it requires compromises, extensive discussions, and often leads to options that don’t really make sense, but everyone can agree on
* Probably the best alternative for irreversible decisions

### Collaborative
* You need a decision maker who’s required to gather input from relevant people, and then come up with a decision
* Strikes the best balance in product
* It is about identifying the most reasonable option to move forward
