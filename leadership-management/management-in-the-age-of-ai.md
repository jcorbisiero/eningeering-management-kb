# Management in the Age of AI

## Changes
Here are the biggest themes of what’s going on right now:
- AI is both making people less collaborative, and driving more need for collaboration
- AI is making the focus on ROI and actual business outcomes stronger than ever
- AI is confusing managers about how to think about performance management
- AI is requiring people to set new standards and culture

## Collaboration
AI has this magic capacity to convince you that you are right or have all the answers. It causes people to not collaborate with others because you can move "faster". This does not mean the answer is always the right answer, but you get an answer faster.

We need to redefine what collaboration is in the age of AI. A simple example is define what a SLA on a PR review looks like.

## Performance
The short playbook is:
- We are raising the bar, everywhere
- We don’t care if you used AI or not as long as the outcome was there
- You own what you ship, no matter who or what wrote it
- The ROI actually needs to be there

What does a raised bar actually look like? 
- For a senior engineer, it means the project that used to take three people now takes one person with a clear spec
- It means “I’m blocked on the frontend team” stops being an acceptable answer for a small change you could make yourself
- It means the design doc shows up with a working prototype attached.

Ownership cuts both ways and you own what you ship. “The AI wrote that part” is not an excuse for a bug, the same way “the intern wrote that part” never was.

## Standards / Culture
Can docs be written with AI? What counts as slop?

New cultural standards are needed in the AI era, and there is strong debate on what is right and what is wrong. IMHO, many of these debates are emotional. 

If you want one rule to follow it’s this: ignore the genesis of output, criticize the content of output.

Who cares if you used AI? Everyone is. Set a culture where criticizing outputs on their own merits, not on their origin, as the norm. This will help people actually understand and articulate what good/bad looks like, and also avoid an anti-AI bias that is increasingly ludicrous.
