### How this key person remains hidden
* Heroics get rewarded
  * The person who swoops in at midnight to fix the thing only they understand gets praised and promoted
  * The colleague who spent that same week writing the runbook that would have prevented the midnight call gets nothing, because you cannot celebrate an outage that never happened

* Redundancy acts as a waste of time
  * Training a backup takes time
  * Preparing someone to protect against a loss that may never happen
  * This is the same reasoning that leads a comfortable organization to cut its safety and sensing functions in a good year, and to rebuild them in a hurry after a bad one

### When the key person becomes a target
* As leaders we are obligated to support the people carrying the load, and build more people who can carry it
* Your strongest people become your load-bearing people because competence attracts responsibility, and you keep handing the hard work to whoever handles it best until the organization is built around one person’s continued presence
* Stewardship means the organization survives their departure, their illness, or their burnout

### How to find the key risk person
* Run an a audit
  * Sit with your team and ask, for every critical system and process, how many people could do this if the primary were gone tomorrow. Where the answer is one, write the name down.

* Run the vacation test
  * Two weeks off the grid is the cheapest test you own. If the operation degrades while someone is on a beach, you found a single point of failure at the best possible price, since the person is still reachable if it gets bad enough.

* Reward the fireproofers
  * Reward the documentation, pairing, cross-training and deliberately unglamorous work that makes the organization less dependent on any one person. If recognition only follows heroics, you are teaching your best people to keep what they know to themselves.

* Make redundancy a design principle
  * Rotate responsibilities
  * Pair people on critical systems
  * Require that important knowledge lives in writing and in a second head
