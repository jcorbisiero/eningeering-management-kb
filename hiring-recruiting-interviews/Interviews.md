### Great questions to ask in an interview

* What is the problem you are trying to solve?  
* What's the hardest problem you are trying to solve right?  
* Are there any problems you are ignoring?  
* What is the biggest complaint your users have right now?  
* How do you attract and retain strong hires?  
* How do you compensate and reward your teams?  
* Do you share board notes/slides with the team?  
* What are the company values and why?  
  * Can you give me examples of how you have followed them?  
* Who decides what to build?  
* What does a typical day look like?  
* What are behaviors that would cause a person to fail in this role?  
* What are you most proud of so far?  
* If you had infinite time and resources, what would you do differently?  
* Can you walk me through the last time someone in this team got promoted? What did the promotion look like, and how long had they been here?  
* What’s the longest someone in this role has been at the company, and what are they doing now?  
  * Follow up with what are they doing now?  
* What’s something the team is genuinely bad at?  
* How does the team handle disagreement with a decision from leadership?  
* What’s the headcount plan for this team over the next twelve months?  
* Who would I be working with most closely, and can I speak with them as part of the process?  
* What does success in this role look like at the six-month mark? And what does failure look like?  
* When was the last reorg, and what triggered it?  
* Why is this role open?  
  * Follow up with Did the previous person move internally or leave the company?

### Resume

Reputational wins that are specific and hard-earned

* A vague “I led a transformation at X” is worth almost nothing. A specific “I rebuilt the credit underwriting model at Bank X, reduced default rates by Y%, and the team that does it now is the team I built” is worth a great deal.
